Miniforge — the factory, drawn twice.

docs/architecture/diagrams · six panels · one palette and legend

Two suites, one palette and legend. Solid strokes mean the thing exists and works; dashed means partial or scaffold; dotted means spec-only. The point of drawing both architectures side by side is the delta — the last panel carries it explicitly, along with the known contradictions the design reviews have on record.

The six panels

As-built (A1–A3): Miniforge is a Clojure Polylith workspace — bases, component subsystems and deployable projects. The core loop drives spec in, PR out: a work-spec kanban feeds a workflow FSM, a DAG orchestrator schedules phases, phase interceptors run agents against LLM backends, and gates collect evidence on the way to release. Target (T1–T3): five planes — factory engine, operator console, Fleet, Data Foundry, evaluation seam — with the run hierarchy, the loop structure, and the built-vs-unbuilt delta.

The architecture, simplified

The same machine, told as three plain-language stories in one shared world — so a human can grok it before (or instead of) reading the drawings. Together they are the simplicity invariant: if a future change to the architecture cannot be told in the story, the change is suspect.

  1. The Fort-Building Robots — the main architecture: the garage board, the wiggle test, the two notebooks, the porch.
  2. The Robot Helpers and the Sticker Rules — agent policy and routing: request slips, stickers, the no-ears doorkeeper, and picking which helper gets the job.
  3. Your House, Their Clubhouse, and the Passes — tenancy, ownership and rights: whose things are whose, passes instead of moved houses, and the moving-box promise.

Why keep the delta on record

Most architecture documents describe an idealized system that the code quietly stopped resembling. These are maintained as a pair on purpose: the as-built suite is corrected against source, the target suite cites its specs, and the delta panel records what is unbuilt and where the design reviews found contradictions. When the factory changes itself, the drawings change with it — through the same pipeline as any other change.
