Ariadne — whose data is whose.

docs/architecture · five panels · one legend across all five

Ariadne gave Theseus the thread that let him walk into the labyrinth and get back out. This architecture is that thread: provenance and authority traceable in both directions, and a guaranteed way out — revocation, takeaway export, leaving day. It was distilled in the Theseus engine and first implemented for Thesium, but the model is engine-agnostic on purpose and is written to be instantiated anywhere people and organizations both hold data.

The problem it solves

Most systems start with one identity: a user string that is simultaneously who is operating, who the data is about, and how rows are keyed. That conflation is invisible while every operator is also the subject of their own data, and becomes structural debt the moment an admin, a manager, a coach or a support engineer appears — or the moment offboarding has to answer "what leaves with the person and what stays with the org" with something better than a policy document.

The usual industry answer is org-centric: the workspace owns everything inside it. That fails any product whose value proposition includes the person's data outlives the membership, and it is unavailable entirely in bottom-up adoption, where no org entity exists yet.

Three axioms

  1. People are tenants. There is no separate subject or user identity layer; the person a record is about is a personal tenant.
  2. Every record has exactly one owner tenant. Anything a second party may see is a granted relation, not a second owner.
  3. Membership is a relation, never containment. Joining writes one tuple; leaving deletes it. No data moves in either direction — which is why offboarding is not a data migration.

Two things that are commonly conflated

Disclosure is not access control. A record marked confidential says what the record permits regardless of who asks; a relation tuple says who may ask. A manager relation does not read a confidential record, and a public record inside the wrong boundary is still unreachable without a relation path. Modeling tenancy as visibility tiers on a confidentiality enum fails exactly here.

Ownership is not visibility. Ownership answers which tenant controls a record's lifecycle, namespace, grants and default portability. It never encodes who may see it.

The architecture, simplified

Two stories, one shared world — the same house, the same doorkeeper, the same stickers. Together they are the simplicity invariant: if a future change to the architecture cannot be told in the story, the change is suspect.

  1. Your House, Their Clubhouse, and the Passes — tenancy, ownership and rights: whose things are whose, passes instead of moved houses, and the moving-box promise.
  2. The Robot Helpers and the Sticker Rules — agents, clauses, the no-ears doorkeeper, and routing.

Where it is used

Ariadne is the ownership model behind the Thesium product line, whose products are local-first by construction — the single-person install and the multi-org hosted deployment are the same model with different tuples, not two codebases. It is written to be adopted incrementally by a system that has the one-string shape today.
